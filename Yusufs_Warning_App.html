      Problem and Zero Hunger relevance
      
    Hunger arrives slowly, then all at once
    
      
    Food shortages build up over months. First the rains fail, then harvests shrink, then market prices climb. By the time families feel it at mealtimes, it is too late to prepare.
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Driest on record
    
          
    In Karamoja, June to September 2026 was the driest second half of the rainy season on record. Most areas received about 200 mm less rain than average.
    
          
    Source: FEWS NET Uganda Food Security Outlook Update, August 2026
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Crisis to January 2027
    
          
    FEWS NET expects Crisis-level food insecurity (IPC Phase 3) to continue across Karamoja through January 2027.
    
          
    Source: FEWS NET, August 2026
    
        
    
        
    
          
    600,000 people
    
          
    In 2024, all nine Karamoja districts were classified in Crisis. About 600,000 people, 45% of those analysed, faced high levels of acute food insecurity.
    
          
    Source: IPC acute food insecurity analysis for Karamoja, June 2024
    
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Who is affected
    
      
    
        
    Farming families
    They eat what they grow. A failed season means an empty grain store.
    
        
    Herding families in Karamoja
    Drought hits their crops and their animals at the same time.
    
        
    Poor households in towns
    They buy all their food. When maize prices double, meals get smaller.
    
        
    Children and mothers
    They are the first to suffer from hunger and malnutrition.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Why an early warning matters
    
      
    
      
    SDG 2, Zero Hunger, aims to end hunger and make sure everyone has enough safe, nutritious food all year round. Warning early turns a crisis into something a community can plan for.

      STEM knowledge and application
      
    From data to action
    
      
    
        
    1. CollectRainfall (mm), and a price and crop condition for each food in the basket
    
        
    2. CompareRain vs normal for that month. Each food's price vs the same month last year, then the basket average.
    
        
    3. Score1 point for each warning sign. Total 0 to 3.
    
        
    4. Alert level0 Normal · 1 Watch · 2 Warning · 3 Crisis
    
        
    5. ActSMS, radio and mosque announcement with the recommended action
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    Why we watch a basket of foods, not just maize
    
      
    
        
    
          
    Families do not eat only maize. When one food gets expensive, many families switch to another food that is cheaper. So watching only maize can get hunger wrong in two ways.
    
          
      
            
    • It can miss hunger. Matooke is an important food in Central Uganda, and sorghum in Karamoja. If those get expensive while maize stays cheap, a maize-only warning stays quiet.
      • 
            
    • It can exaggerate hunger. If only maize jumps but other foods stay cheap, families can switch, so the danger is smaller than maize alone suggests.
      • 
          
    
          
    We never mix units. Each food is compared only with itself a year ago, in the same unit: a kilo with a kilo, a bunch with a bunch. That gives a percentage change. Percentages have no units, so we can average them fairly.
    
          
    Crops: the crop sign scores 1 point if half or more of the foods we track have a Poor crop, or if the region's main staple has a Poor crop.
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Example with made-up numbers
    
          
    
            
    FoodChange on last year
    
            
              
    Maize+30%
    
              
    Beans+10%
    
              
    Matooke+5%
    
              
    Basket average(30 + 10 + 5) ÷ 3 = 15%
    
                
          
    
          
    Maize only: +30% is above the 20% limit, so 1 point.
    Food basket: +15% is below the limit, so 0 points. Families can switch to beans and matooke for now.

    How one month is stored
    

    { y: 2025, m: 3, rain: 110,
  foods: {
    maize: { price: 1300, unit: 'kg', crop: 'Fair' },
    beans: { price: 4650, unit: 'kg', crop: 'Fair' },
    matooke: { price: 23900, unit: 'bunch', crop: 'Fair' }
  } }
    
          
    m: 3 means April, because JavaScript counts from 0 (January is 0). To find the same month last year, the code turns each month into one number, year × 12 + month, and subtracts 12.
    
        
    
      
    
      
    Worked example: April 2025 with three foods
    
      
    Illustrative sample data, basket of maize, beans and cassava, main staple maize. An automatic test checks that the code gives these same numbers.
    
      
    
        
    Rain: 110 mm. Normal for April is 175 mm.
    80% × 175 = 140. Is 110 < 140? Yes → 1 point
    
        
    Prices: each food compared with April 2024, in the same unit.
    
          
    
            
    FoodApr 2025Apr 2024Change
    
            
              
    Maize1,3001,060(1,300 − 1,060) ÷ 1,060 = +22.6%
    
              
    Beans4,6503,750(4,650 − 3,750) ÷ 3,750 = +24.0%
    
              
    Cassava1,2801,050(1,280 − 1,050) ÷ 1,050 = +21.9%
    
                
          
    
          
    Basket = (22.6 + 24.0 + 21.9) ÷ 3 = 22.8%. Is 22.8% > 20%? Yes → 1 point
    
        
    Crops: maize Fair, beans Fair, cassava Good. 0 of 3 Poor.
    Is 0 ≥ 1.5 (half of 3)? No. Is maize (main staple) Poor? No → 0 points
    
        
    Total: 1 + 1 + 0 = 2 → !! Warning. March 2025 was Watch, so the level rose to Warning and an alert is sent. Beans rose fastest, so the SMS names beans.
    
      
    
      
    10 questions judges could ask about the code
    
      
    
        
    1. Which languages did you use, and why no libraries?
    HTML for the page, CSS for the look, JavaScript for the logic. With no libraries, everything is in one file that works offline and that we can explain line by line.
    
        
    2. Where is the data saved? Is it sent anywhere?
    It is saved in the browser on this device using localStorage. Nothing is sent to a server. To back it up, we press "Copy current data as CSV".
    
        
    3. Where in the code is the alert level decided?
    In the section called THE SCORING RULES. The function analyseWith adds up rainSign, priceSign and cropSign to get a score from 0 to 3, then picks Normal, Watch, Warning or Crisis.
    
        
    4. How does the code find the same month last year?
    It turns each month into one number: year × 12 + month. April 2025 is 2025 × 12 + 3 = 24303. The same month last year is 24303 − 12 = 24291, which is April 2024.
    
        
    5. How can you average a price per kg and a price per bunch?
    We never average prices. Each food is compared only with itself in the same unit, which gives a percentage change. Percentages have no units, so we can average them. If a food's unit changed, the code will not compare the old and new prices.
    
        
    6. What happens if a food has no price for last year?
    The code compares it with the average of its 3 months before. If those are missing too, that food is left out of the basket this month. There are automatic tests for both cases.
    
        
    7. Why is 80 mm not a warning sign when the limit is 80 mm?
    The rule uses "less than" (<), not "less than or equal". Rain exactly at the limit is not a warning sign. We test exactly at the limit and just below it.
    
        
    8. Why do you round to one decimal place?
    So that the code gets the same answer as a person working it out by hand. Computers also store some decimals slightly wrong (0.1 + 0.2 gives 0.30000000000000004), and rounding stops that tiny error from changing a result.
    
        
    9. Why doesn't the app send an alert every month that is at Warning?
    The function buildLog only sends an alert when the level rises and reaches Warning or Crisis. If people got the same alert every month, they would stop paying attention.
    
        
    10. How do you know the code works?
    The Testing tab runs the automatic tests through the same scoring functions every time the app opens, including edge cases like values exactly at the limit. We also compare our alerts with FEWS NET phases in the backtest.
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    The STEM inside the project
    
      
    
        
    SScience
    Crops need water for photosynthesis and growth. Too little rain at the wrong time means a poor harvest. Less food in markets pushes prices up (supply and demand).
    
        
    TTechnology
    A web app built with HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It saves data in the browser, draws its own charts and works offline.
    
        
    EEngineering
    We followed the design process: define the problem, design, build, test, then improve. See the version history on the Testing tab.
    
        
    MMathematics
    Percentages, averages, percentage change, thresholds and inequalities (is the value above or below the limit?).

    Backtest: did our alerts match FEWS NET?
    
        
      
    
      
    FEWS NET and the IPC give each area a food security phase for each season: 1 Minimal, 2 Stressed, 3 Crisis, 4 Emergency, 5 Famine. Look up the phases for your region on fews.net and enter one row per season. The app then compares its own alerts with them, month by month.

        How we count hits, misses and false alarms
        
      
          
    • We only count months that have a phase in the table.
      • 
          
    • A real crisis means FEWS NET says Phase 3 (Crisis) or worse in that month or in the next 2 months. We look 2 months ahead because an early warning should come before the crisis, not during it.
      • 
          
    • The app warned means the app was at Warning or Crisis that month.
      • 
          
    • Hit: the app warned and a crisis came. Miss: a crisis came but the app did not warn. False alarm: the app warned but no crisis came. Correct normal: no warning and no crisis.
      • 
          
    • "Maize only" uses the same rules but watches maize alone, like version 4 of our app.
      • 
        
    
      
    
    
    

    
    
      
    How the thresholds change the result
    
      
    Number of months at Warning or Crisis for different rain and price limits, with the first warning month in brackets. Our chosen setting is highlighted.
    
      
    
      
    Stricter limits give fewer false alarms but risk missing a real crisis. Looser limits warn more often. Choosing thresholds is a trade-off.

    Limitations we know about
    
        
      
          
    • The sample replay data is made up. Real results depend on real data.
      • 
          
    • Only three warning signs. Professional systems also use vegetation from satellites and livestock condition.
      • 
          
    • Data is typed in by hand once a month, so mistakes are possible.
      • 
          
    • Crop condition (Good, Fair, Poor) for each food is a judgement, not a measurement.
      • 
          
    • The basket counts every food equally unless we set weights. Real diets differ from family to family.
      • 
          
    • The region presets are suggestions we still need to check with local people.
      • 
          
    • One region and one market at a time.
      • 
          
    • The app does not send SMS by itself. A person copies and shares the alert.

      Islamic perspective
      
    Feeding others and avoiding waste
    
      
    
        
    "You will plant for seven years consecutively; and what you harvest leave in its spikes, except a little from which you will eat."Surah Yusuf 12:47. Prophet Yusuf (AS) read the warning signs and planned ahead. That is our model.
    
        
    "And they give food in spite of love for it to the needy, the orphan, and the captive."Surah Al-Insan 76:8. Feeding others is a mark of the righteous.
    
        
    "Eat and drink, but be not excessive. Indeed, He likes not those who commit excess."Surah Al-A'raf 7:31. Avoiding Israf: no waste in good times.
    
        
    "He is not a believer whose stomach is filled while his neighbour goes hungry."Hadith, Al-Adab al-Mufrad 112. Our responsibility reaches our neighbours.
    
      
    
      
    Translations: Sahih International. Check wording with your Islamic studies teacher.
